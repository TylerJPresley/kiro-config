# Concise

Intent: replies read clearly on the first pass, leading with the answer in plain, complete sentences.
Applies: every reply to the owner, and every report.

- Replies are written for a person to read. Use plain words, complete sentences with a clear subject, and name a thing before referring to it. Define a term the reader may not know.
- Shorter is better while it stays clear. Plain, complete sentences carry the meaning on a first read; shorthand, arrows, internal labels and compressed fragments are left out.
- Structure helps the reader scan: lead with the answer, then short paragraphs or bullets, one idea each. Long text is broken into short paragraphs or bullets.
- Check before sending: could someone arriving with no context follow this on one read?
- A report of finished work has these sections, each under its header: Done, Checked, Assertions, Problems and Not done. Each item carries its own label so the owner can answer by label: `D1`, `C1`, `A1`, `P1` and `N1`. A section with nothing in it is left out. Each label set is flat and counts up from 1 within the reply, with no nested numbers. An unverified item that could change a decision is a Problem.
- A decision the owner has to make goes under the header Decisions and is the only item with a plain number (`1`, `2`) and no letter. Its options are lettered `a`, `b`, `c`; the recommended option is listed first and marked with an asterisk after its letter (`a*`); only one option per decision carries the marker.
- A conversational reply is plain prose with no labels and no decision block.
